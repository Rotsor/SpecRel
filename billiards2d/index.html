How the causal model works

Every ball stores its exact 4-momentum (E, px, py); its rest mass is √(E²−p²) and its velocity p/E. All balls coast in lockstep in the centre-of-momentum (CM) frame.

When two balls first touch, at event P (found exactly within the step), the collision is solved in the pair's own CM frame: elastic contacts reverse the momentum component along the contact normal, sticky ones leave both balls co-moving with a rest-mass share of the total. The two impulses cancel exactly.

But neither ball may feel its impulse until P is in the causal past of every point of it (|P−centre| + r ≤ t − tP): there are no rigid bodies in relativity. Until then a ball coasts on and overlaps what it hit, like a compressible body. The violet circles are the light fronts from P; a ball changes course once the front has swept over all of it. A push therefore can't run down a line of balls faster than light.

While an impulse is pending, its 4-momentum is "in transit": it has left the balls' sum but not arrived. Conservation holds for balls + transit, to rounding error.

A ball can be hit again before it has felt the first hit (a rack, a pile-up). The new collision is then solved with its effective momentum: felt plus still-pending impulses. The earlier contacts become dependencies, and nobody feels the new impulse until their light fronts have crossed them too. So a ball always passes through states of an ordinary chain of two-body collisions: elastic contacts never change rest mass, and heat (rest-mass gain) comes only from sticky contacts.

Simplification: balls are drawn and collided as circles in the CM frame, not Lorentz-contracted ellipses.
